01Executive Summary

What HOC is, and why this contract needs it.

Highway Operations Command (HOC) is a digital operations platform purpose-built for organisations that hold highway maintenance contracts with national or state authorities such as NHAI. It replaces today's WhatsApp-and-phone-call coordination with a structured, auditable, geographically-aware system of record for every incident, work order, and field action across the 200 km of highway under contract.

HOC is not a generic project-management tool. It is closer in spirit to a field-service dispatch platform combined with an infrastructure asset register and a GIS workflow engine. The system's centre of gravity is the physical highway itself — every event, every photo, every signature is anchored to a chainage point on the road and to the engineer responsible for that stretch.

What the system does

  • Captures every maintenance incident — pothole, drain blockage, damaged barrier, fallen signboard, waterlogging — with GPS coordinates, photo evidence, and timestamp.
  • Converts incidents into work orders, assigns them to the right engineer based on the 50 km stretch they own, and mobilises supervisors, workers, materials, and equipment.
  • Tracks execution in the field, on a phone, with offline capability so connectivity gaps on remote stretches don't break the workflow.
  • Verifies completion with before / during / after photo evidence, GPS-stamped, and a QA / Safety sign-off.
  • Surfaces SLA breaches, escalations, and engineer performance to the owner and NHAI through a dashboard and exportable reports.

Why it matters

TodayWith HOC
Work tracked in notebooks & WhatsAppSingle system of record
No proof of completionGPS + photo + timestamp evidence
SLA compliance is anecdotalSLA clock per work order, breach alerts

02Business Context

The operation, the work, and the problems HOC solves.

The operation

The business holds a maintenance contract for approximately 200 km of national highway in India. The highway is divided into stretches of roughly 50 km each, with one Zone Engineer accountable per stretch. The contract obliges the business to keep the highway safe, drivable, and aesthetically maintained — and to respond to incidents within agreed SLAs.

The work

Highway maintenance is dynamic and event-driven. Unlike a construction project with a fixed scope, every day brings a different mix of work generated by weather, traffic, accidents, and natural deterioration.

The operating problems HOC solves

No single source of truth

Work lives in WhatsApp, paper logs, and engineer memory. "Was that pothole near km 142 fixed?" — nobody can answer with confidence.

No proof of work

When NHAI asks for evidence of completion, the team scrambles to find photos.

No SLA visibility

Breaches are discovered only when the authority complains.

No productivity measurement

Impossible to know whether Engineer A is genuinely more productive than Engineer B, or just better at communicating.

No structured escalation

A stuck work order has no defined path upward — it festers until it becomes a crisis.

No audit trail

The contract is auditable; the operation is not.

Ten end-to-end workflows. Each one: Trigger → Role → Inputs → Outputs → SLA → Failure modes.

4.1Incident identification

Trigger
Daily patrol observation, public complaint, NHAI notification, CCTV/camera alert, or scheduled inspection.
Role
Zone Engineer or Supervisor (most common); occasionally Owner forwarding a complaint.
Inputs
GPS location (auto-captured), chainage (km marker), photo, free-text description, category tag, severity.
Outputs
An Incident record with a unique ID, geo-tagged and time-stamped.
SLA
Log within 30 min
Failure
No mobile signal → offline capture, sync later. Wrong chainage → snap-to-segment using highway centreline. Duplicates → de-duplication by proximity + category.

4.2Work order creation

Trigger
An Incident is triaged.
Role
Zone Engineer.
Inputs
The Incident, work category, estimated effort, estimated materials, target date, assigned supervisor.
Outputs
A Work Order linked to the Incident; SLA clock starts.
SLA
Open within 4 hrs (routine) · immediate (emergency)
Failure
Engineer overloaded → workload visible to Owner who can re-balance. Missing materials → flagged before mobilisation.

4.3Engineer assignment & workforce mobilisation

Trigger
Work order is open.
Role
Zone Engineer assigns; Supervisor mobilises.
Inputs
Work order, available crew, equipment, materials, traffic conditions.
Outputs
A Mobilisation record: who is going, with what, when, ETA on site.
SLA
On-site within window per category
Failure
Crew diverted to emergency → WO re-scheduled with reason. No equipment → escalation to Owner.

4.4Material & equipment allocation

Trigger
Mobilisation planning.
Role
Supervisor, validated by Engineer.
Inputs
Material stock levels, equipment availability roster.
Outputs
A Material Issue record (what left the stockyard) and an Equipment Booking.
SLA
Before crew dispatch
Failure
Stockout → procurement workflow triggered, WO paused with reason.

4.5Site execution

Trigger
Crew arrives on site.
Role
Supervisor leads, workers execute.
Inputs
Work order, safety checklist, traffic management plan.
Outputs
Before photo (mandatory), during photo(s), after photo (mandatory), GPS check-in/out, worker attendance, material consumed.
SLA
Duration per category benchmark
Failure
Weather stoppage → pause with reason. Safety incident → escalation triggered.

4.6Quality verification

Trigger
Supervisor marks work "ready for QA".
Role
QA / Safety Officer.
Inputs
After photos, GPS evidence, work-order specification.
Outputs
Inspection record — pass, fail, or conditional pass with remarks.
SLA
Review within 24 hrs
Failure
Fail → WO re-opened, returned to Supervisor with notes.

4.7Closure

Trigger
QA pass.
Role
Zone Engineer signs off; Owner has visibility.
Inputs
QA-passed work order, evidence pack.
Outputs
WO closed, SLA clock stopped, Incident marked resolved.
SLA
Closure within 24 hrs of QA pass
Failure
Disputed closure → flagged for Owner review.

4.8Escalation handling

Trigger
SLA breach imminent or breached; safety issue; resource shortage; QA fail count exceeds threshold.
Role
Automatic system trigger; Owner is final escalation point.
Inputs
Work order, breach reason, SLA history.
Outputs
Escalation record with severity, owner, and resolution timer.
SLA
L1 Engineer 2h · L2 Owner 4h · L3 Authority 24h
Failure
Escalation ignored → auto-promotion to next tier.

4.9Emergency response

Trigger
Accident, fallen tree, large pothole risk, oil spill, waterlogging, animal hazard.
Role
Whoever spots it logs "Emergency"; nearest crew dispatched.
Inputs
Location, hazard type, photo.
Outputs
Immediate dispatch; WO generated after site action.
SLA
First responder on site < 60 min · hazard cleared < 2 hrs
Failure
No nearby crew → traffic police / emergency services notified through the system.

4.10Reporting

Trigger
Daily, weekly, monthly cadence; on-demand for NHAI.
Role
Owner / Contract Manager.
Inputs
All work orders, evidence, SLA performance.
Outputs
Daily ops summary, weekly engineer scorecard, monthly NHAI report pack (PDF with photo evidence).
SLA
Monthly report by 5th of following month
Failure
Incomplete evidence → flagged before report generation.

Status lifecycles

Incident

reported → triaged → linked-to-WO → resolved → closed
Exits: duplicate, invalid

Work Order

open → planned → mobilised → in-progress → ready-for-qa → qa-pass → closed
Other paths: paused, qa-fail → in-progress, cancelled

Mobilisation

planned → in-transit → on-site → completed → returned

Inspection / Escalation

pending → pass | fail | conditional-pass
Escalation: raised → acknowledged → in-progress → resolved → closed (auto-promotes on timeout)

Geospatial & audit requirements

Geospatial: PostGIS storage; GeoJSON interchange; MapLibre/Mapbox GL visualisation. Distance and containment queries ("which segment does this incident fall on?") are everyday operations.

Audit: Every transactional entity carries created_by, created_at, updated_by, updated_at, and an immutable append-only change log. Photo evidence stores original EXIF alongside server-recorded values to detect tampering.

06Engagement Channels & UX

Mobile-first because the work is in the field. The web dashboard is for the Owner and for reporting — not for daily execution.

Channel matrix

ChannelPrimary userPrimary use

Supervisor mobile appSupervisorMobilise crew, capture evidence, log materials
Worker mobile app (simplified)Field WorkerCheck in on site, take photos, mark task done
QA mobile appQA / SafetyInspect, pass/fail, capture remarks

Key screens

Dashboard (Web — Owner)

Live map with incident pins, SLA breach count, open/closed WOs, engineer load, today's mobilisations, emergency feed. Drill into segment, engineer, or WO; export report.

Incident reporting (Mobile)

Capture a new incident in under 60 seconds. Auto GPS, chainage auto-snap, category icon picker, mandatory photo, voice-to-text description. Offline-capable with pending-sync badge.

Work order detail

Linked incident, category, assigned supervisor, materials, equipment, SLA clock, status, evidence gallery, audit log. Assign, mobilise, pause, resume, mark ready-for-QA, close.

Map-based tracking

Segments coloured by SLA health, incident pins by severity, live vehicle/crew positions, asset overlays. Filter by category, status, engineer.

SLA monitoring (Web)

Open WOs by SLA bucket (green/amber/red), breaches this week, breach reasons, repeat-breach segments. Drill to WO; raise escalation.

Engineer performance (Web)

Per-engineer scorecard: WOs closed, avg closure time, SLA compliance %, QA pass rate, evidence completeness %, emergency response time.

Daily work log (Mobile)

End-of-day "what happened in my zone". Today's incidents, mobilisations, closures, escalations, planned tomorrow. Sign off the day.

Photo upload workflow

Phase tag (before/during/after), auto GPS, auto timestamp, WO link. In-app camera only — no gallery upload, to prevent fake evidence. Offline-queued, retried on reconnect.

Emergency response

Fastest path from "I see a hazard" to "crew dispatched". Single-tap raise — notifies nearest crew + engineer + owner via push + SMS.

Escalation management

Make stuck work visible. Tier (L1/L2/L3), reason, age, owner, target resolution. Acknowledge, reassign, resolve, auto-promote on timeout.

Mobile-first design principles

  • One-thumb operations. Field users wear gloves, work in sun, hold a torch.
  • Big targets, high contrast. Outdoor readability is non-negotiable.
  • Voice input for descriptions; icons for categories.
  • Offline by default. Connectivity on a highway is unreliable.
  • Battery-aware GPS. Active (30 s) only on mobilisation; passive otherwise.
  • Minimal typing. Pickers, presets, and auto-fill everywhere.

Offline synchronisation

The mobile apps treat the local SQLite store as the source of truth for the user's current session. Reads serve from local cache; writes go to a local outbox. A background sync worker drains the outbox on connectivity (Wi-Fi preferred), resolves conflicts with last-write-wins per field plus a server-enforced state machine (so a worker can't "close" something the engineer just re-opened), pulls a delta for the user's assigned scope, and uploads photos through a chunked, resumable uploader.

GPS tracking

  • Foreground (worker on a mobilisation): every 30 s, accuracy ≤ 10 m.
  • Background (app open, no active mobilisation): every 5 min, low accuracy.
  • Off (app closed, no mobilisation): no tracking — for privacy and battery.
  • All tracks stored as line geometries with timestamps in PostGIS; replayable in the dashboard.

Photo evidence integrity

  • Captured only through the in-app camera (no gallery picker).
  • Server reads EXIF: GPS, timestamp, device, orientation.
  • Server compares EXIF GPS with current device GPS at submission.
  • Compressed to ≤ 800 KB for mobile data; high-res original retained for evidence packs.
  • Image hash stored to detect later tampering.

Service boundary principles

  • One service per bounded context from the operational model.
  • Each service owns its database tables — no shared schema across services.
  • Cross-service communication via events; synchronous APIs only where strictly necessary.
  • Start as a modular monolith (one deployable, clear module boundaries); split when scale or team structure demands it.

API style

  • REST + JSON for the bulk of CRUD interactions.
  • WebSocket for live updates (map, dashboard).
  • OpenAPI specs as the contract; SDKs generated for mobile and web.
  • Pagination, filtering, sorting standardised across endpoints.
  • Idempotency keys on all mutating endpoints — critical for offline sync retry.

11Phased Roadmap

Three phases over twelve months — from one zone to one platform.

Phase 1 · Months 0–3

Foundation. One zone (50 km), one engineer, end-to-end core flow working.

Highway segment & asset import · basic admin · mobile app for incident → WO → evidence → closure · web dashboard map & WO list · offline sync · photo evidence · GPS · auth & roles.

Phase 2 · Months 3–6

Operationalise. Whole 200 km, all roles, SLA & escalation live.

SLA engine, escalation ladder, notifications (push + SMS) · QA workflow · engineer performance dashboard · monthly NHAI report pack · contractor module · material & equipment tracking.

Phase 3 · Months 6–12

Intelligence. From record-keeping to decision support.

Predictive maintenance signals · AI-assisted incident categorisation · asset health scoring · multi-contract support · NHAI external read-only view.

12Success Metrics

What v1 success looks like, in numbers.

  • 100% of incidents logged in the system — zero WhatsApp coordination for tracked work.
  • 100% of closed work orders have before + after photo evidence.
  • SLA compliance ≥ 90% measurable in the dashboard (today: unmeasurable).
  • Monthly NHAI report generated from the system in under 1 hour (today: 2–3 days).
  • Engineer admin time reduced by 30% — more time on the road.
  • Audit response reduced from days to minutes.

13What This System Is Not

Saying no clearly is how the core flow stays the core flow.

  • Not a generic project-management tool (Jira / Trello / Asana).
  • Not a chat / collaboration tool — WhatsApp can stay for informal chat; the system is for the work record.
  • Not an HR / payroll system — workforce is modelled only to the extent needed for dispatch and accountability.
  • Not a procurement system — material is tracked as consumption, not as a supply chain.
  • Not an ERP — financials live elsewhere; HOC exposes the operational data they need.
